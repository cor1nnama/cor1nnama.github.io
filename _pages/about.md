I am a graduate student at the **University of Southern California**, currently pursuing an M.S. in Analytics. My research interests lie at the intersection of **algorithmic game theory, computational social choice, combinatorial optimization, and theoretical computer science**.

My current research at USC focuses on algorithmic decision-making and voting, including metric distortion, robustness to misinformation, liquid democracy, and lower-bound constructions for voting rules. I also work on approximation algorithms and combinatorial optimization, and I am interested broadly in how mathematical structure can be exploited to obtain rigorous guarantees for complex decision problems.

## Research

### Algorithmic Game Theory & Computational Social Choice

With **Prof. David Kempe**, I study algorithmic decision-making and computational social choice. Current work examines **metric distortion of voting rules**, including lower-bound constructions for **Single Transferable Vote (STV)** when voters may be misinformed about preferences. I use combinatorial constructions, linear programming duality, flow arguments, and structural analysis of voting procedures to derive theoretical guarantees.

### Combinatorial Optimization & Algorithms

With **Prof. John Gunnar Carlsson**, I study approximation algorithms and optimization problems involving clustering, graphs, and matching. My interests include designing algorithms with provable guarantees and understanding the structural properties that make difficult discrete optimization problems tractable.

### Optimization & Numerical Methods

With **Prof. Satish Kumar Thittamaranahalli**, I work on optimization-based numerical methods for differential equations, including quadratic programming formulations and Bézier curve parameterizations for ODE solution and stability analysis.
