My research interests lie broadly in **algorithmic game theory, computational social choice, combinatorial optimization, and algorithm design**. I am particularly interested in understanding how theoretical guarantees change when classical assumptions about information and rationality are relaxed.

## Algorithmic Game Theory & Computational Social Choice

### Metric Distortion under Misinformed Preferences

*Research with Prof. David Kempe, University of Southern California*

I study **metric distortion in social choice under misinformation**, focusing on how the guarantees of voting rules change when voters do not have exact information about their underlying metric preferences.

We consider an **α-misinformation framework**, in which voters' perceived preferences may differ from those induced by the true metric, subject to a multiplicative distortion parameter α. This provides a way to interpolate between the classical metric-preference setting and environments in which voters make decisions using systematically distorted information.

My current work investigates the behavior of voting rules, particularly **Single Transferable Vote (STV)**, under this framework. We study how misinformation affects distortion guarantees and construct metric instances and voting profiles that characterize the dependence of distortion on α and the number of candidates.

More broadly, I am interested in how limited or inaccurate information changes the approximation guarantees achievable by collective decision-making mechanisms.

### Delegation and Liquid Democracy

*Research with Prof. David Kempe, University of Southern California*

I also study algorithmic questions arising in **liquid democracy and delegated voting**. My work investigates optimization and graph-based formulations of delegation systems, using techniques including **linear programming duality, network flows, and combinatorial optimization** to analyze mechanism performance and derive theoretical guarantees.

## Combinatorial Optimization & Algorithms

*Research with Prof. John Gunnar Carlsson, University of Southern California*

I study algorithmic problems in **combinatorial optimization**, including approximation algorithms for clustering and NP-hard graph problems, as well as algorithmic questions related to stable matching.

I am particularly interested in understanding when computationally difficult optimization problems admit efficient algorithms with provable approximation guarantees and how structural properties of an instance can be exploited algorithmically.

## Optimization & Numerical Methods

*Research with Prof. T. K. Satish Kumar, University of Southern California*

I have worked on optimization-based methods for solving and controlling dynamical systems, including formulations that replace repeated simulation with direct mathematical optimization.

One line of this work uses **Bézier-curve parameterizations and quadratic programming** to represent system trajectories and optimize control points. We also investigated Laplace-transform-based formulations for projecting ordinary differential equation constraints into optimization problems.

This research contributed to our paper, **“Bypassing Simulation-Based Methods: A Case Study in HVAC Scheduling,”** published at **ISAIM 2026**.
